Q1

Compute the mean, median, 90th percentile, and standard deviation of arrival delay minutes for RegionEx flights. Do the same for MDA flights.

flightdf <- read.csv("flight_delay_clean.csv")

flightdf %>%
  filter(airline %in% c("RegionEx", "MDA")) %>%
  group_by(airline) %>%
  summarise(
    n = n(),
    mean_delay = mean(delay, na.rm = TRUE),
    median_delay = median(delay, na.rm = TRUE),
    perc_90 = as.numeric(quantile(delay, probs = 0.90, na.rm = TRUE)),
    sd_delay = sd(delay, na.rm = TRUE)
  )
# A tibble: 2 × 6
  airline      n mean_delay median_delay perc_90 sd_delay
  <chr>    <int>      <dbl>        <dbl>   <dbl>    <dbl>
1 MDA        120       10.9           13    16.1     6.34
2 RegionEx   240       15.7            9    21      27.7

Contractual obligations aside, which measure of central tendency would be most appropriate for comparing airline performance?

According to the data, if only one measure can be used, the median is a better measure of central tendency. When looking at the mean vs median of the data for RegionEx its mean is significantly larger than the median. This means there is a few extream outliers that are increasing the average delay time. This can be further explained by including the 90th percentile and the standard deviation. With a very large standard deviation as shown in the RegionEx several outliers are causing a significant increase to the mean.
